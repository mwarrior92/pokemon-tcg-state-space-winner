## Recommended play style

- **Win condition.** Prizes come mostly from Mega Symphonia (Mega Gardevoir ex) – 57% of prizes; Eon Blade (Latias ex) – 25% of prizes; Linear Attack (Cacturne) – 11% of prizes. Build towards Mega Gardevoir ex first.
- **Tempo.** First prize typically on its own turn 4; 0.35 prizes per own turn.
- **Opening (first two turns).** Most games: tool Lucky Helmet (56%), retreat (48%), attach Psychic Energy to Latias ex (47%), attach Psychic Energy to Minccino (44%), bench Ralts (43%).
- **Cards that show up in wins.** Psychic Energy (6.0 uses per won game vs 4.9 per lost game); Mega Gardevoir ex (0.9 uses per won game vs 0.3 per lost game); Gwynn (1.6 uses per won game vs 1.1 per lost game); Lillie's Determination (1.4 uses per won game vs 1.1 per lost game); Lucky Helmet (1.8 uses per won game vs 1.5 per lost game); Kirlia (1.2 uses per won game vs 0.9 per lost game). Prioritise getting these into play / using them.
- **Cards that show up in losses.** Alomomola (1.2 vs 0.9); Minccino (1.3 vs 1.1); Cacturne (0.9 vs 0.7); Cacnea (1.1 vs 1.0) – often a sign of playing from behind with them, or of a weak slot.
- **Where it gets stuck.** Most often "race:behind ready:0-2 ko:no threat:yes exposed:none" (79 turns; still there the next turn 68% of the time). To get out: get a second attacker ready earlier (bench and power up the next attacker before the first is Knocked Out); avoid leaving the Active in Knock Out range (retreat, switch, or put a cheaper Pokémon in front); keep a way to take a prize every turn (gust for weak benched targets, damage spread).
- **Matchups.** Best against Slowking (78%), Dhelmise (75%); weakest against N's Zoroark (0%), Festival Lead (17%).

## How to read this

- Games are full games from turn 1 against meta decks chosen by their share of the decklists, piloted by the same simulator policy on both sides. Set-up choices (opening Active and Bench) and the opponent's prompts during a turn are made at random.
- The result says how well this simulator plays the deck, not only how strong the deck is: decks whose plan relies on long ability chains are likely under-piloted. Compare a new deck with known meta decks tested the same way.
