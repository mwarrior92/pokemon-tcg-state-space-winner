## Recommended play style

- **Win condition.** Prizes come mostly from Mega Symphonia (Mega Gardevoir ex) – 46% of prizes; Full Moon Rondo (Lillie's Clefairy ex) – 26% of prizes; Eon Blade (Latias ex) – 24% of prizes. Build towards Mega Gardevoir ex first.
- **Tempo.** First prize typically on its own turn 4; 0.38 prizes per own turn.
- **Opening (first two turns).** Most games: retreat (51%), tool Lucky Helmet (41%), play Lucian (41%), attack Take Down (38%), attach Psychic Energy to Minccino (34%).
- **Cards that show up in wins.** Psychic Energy (5.2 uses per won game vs 4.2 per lost game); Mega Gardevoir ex (0.8 uses per won game vs 0.3 per lost game); Prism Energy (1.0 uses per won game vs 0.7 per lost game); Kirlia (1.2 uses per won game vs 0.9 per lost game); Telepathic Psychic Energy (0.6 uses per won game vs 0.4 per lost game); Lucian (1.1 uses per won game vs 1.0 per lost game). Prioritise getting these into play / using them.
- **Cards that show up in losses.** Latias ex (0.7 vs 0.3); Minccino (1.2 vs 0.9); Night Stretcher (0.6 vs 0.4); Drifblim (0.2 vs 0.1) – often a sign of playing from behind with them, or of a weak slot.
- **Where it gets stuck.** Most often "race:behind ready:0-2 ko:no threat:yes exposed:none" (73 turns; still there the next turn 59% of the time). To get out: get a second attacker ready earlier (bench and power up the next attacker before the first is Knocked Out); avoid leaving the Active in Knock Out range (retreat, switch, or put a cheaper Pokémon in front); keep a way to take a prize every turn (gust for weak benched targets, damage spread).
- **Matchups.** Best against Slowking (67%), Dragapult Blaziken (50%); weakest against Alakazam Dusknoir (0%), N's Zoroark (0%).

## How to read this

- Games are full games from turn 1 against meta decks chosen by their share of the decklists, piloted by the same simulator policy on both sides. Set-up choices (opening Active and Bench) and the opponent's prompts during a turn are made at random.
- The result says how well this simulator plays the deck, not only how strong the deck is: decks whose plan relies on long ability chains are likely under-piloted. Compare a new deck with known meta decks tested the same way.
